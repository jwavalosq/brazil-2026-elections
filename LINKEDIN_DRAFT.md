Brazil just had a historic election, and I wanted to understand it with data, not rumors.

Brazil votes in two rounds. If nobody gets more than half the votes in round one, the top two go to round two.

In round one, Flavio Bolsonaro led. In round two, Lula won. That had never happened before in Brazil's modern democracy.

So I asked a simple question: where did Lula's 10.4 million new votes come from?

I used official results from all 5,571 towns and cities in Brazil. The math adds up. Votes from candidates who were eliminated, plus millions of people who stayed home in round one and voted in round two, cover the total exactly.

And the gain happened almost everywhere, not in a few strange places. Lula gained votes in 98.6 percent of towns and cities.

Some people say the election was fraud. I tested what the data can test, and I am honest about what it cannot test. Vote totals alone cannot prove an election was clean, and they cannot prove fraud either.
